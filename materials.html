The Materials Behind the Craft.

Steel, wood, metal and natural materials come together to create every handcrafted piece. This page explains their visual and practical characteristics without turning the archive into a manufacturing manual.

01 / Steel

Damascus Steel

In modern craft, “Damascus steel” commonly refers to pattern-welded steel made by combining compatible steels into a layered material. Its recognizable pattern becomes visible through shaping, polishing and controlled surface finishing.

A

Layered Construction

Alternating steel layers create contrast across the surface.

B

Pattern Development

Different working approaches create flowing, laddered or geometric motifs.

C

Heat Treatment

Thermal processing influences the final characteristics of the steel.

D

Surface Finish

Grinding, polishing and etching reveal the visual structure.

02 / Blade Materials

High-Carbon & Stainless Steel

Both families appear across blade-making. Their behavior depends on alloy composition, heat treatment, geometry and finishing.

High-Carbon Steel

Hardness & Character

Often valued for hardness, edge retention and the way its surface develops character over time. Corrosion considerations are part of the material story.

Stainless Steel

Durability & Resistance

Designed with chromium-rich compositions that improve corrosion resistance. Stainless families can also be used in fittings and hybrid blade constructions.

03 / Handle Materials

Wood, Micarta & Natural Materials

Walnut

Warm brown tones, visible grain and a familiar tactile character make walnut a classic handle material.

Rosewood

Deep color and dense grain have made rosewood a traditional choice for decorative and functional handles.

Micarta

Layered composite construction offers a consistent texture and durable visual language.

Stabilized Wood

Wood can be stabilized for a more consistent handle material while preserving natural grain.

Bone / Horn

Historically used for decorative handles and fittings, valued for natural variation and heritage character.
